## Problem

On the owner's Pixel 10, the tweet "growing" on `/journals` was choppy. The owner asked whether the site had simply run out of animation budget on ordinary devices.

It had not. The *number* of animations is not the issue; the *kind* is:
- Animating `transform` or `opacity` is cheap: the GPU moves or fades an already-painted layer.
- The tweet slot animated **`height`** (CSS transition, 460 ms). Every frame re-ran layout for the whole catalog grid (the card grows, the cards below move, iframes resize), once per revealing tweet, with five or more tweets revealing within a couple of seconds. That is main-thread work, and a phone CPU is several times slower than the development laptop.

## Decision

- **FLIP (First, Last, Invert, Play) instead of an animated height.** `flipPush()` in `journal-media.js`:
  1. Records the visual position of every card in the grid, plus the content below the slot inside its own card.
  2. Applies the final slot height in **one** layout.
  3. Offsets everything that moved back with `transform`, and clips every card that grew back to its old height. Only the bottom edge is clipped, so side shadows stay.
  4. Animates the offsets and clips away (Web Animations API, 460 ms, the same easing as before).
  - Overlapping reveals and Twitter's late second resize start from where things *visually* are, because the "first" measurement includes any in-flight offset.
  - Reduced motion applies the layout change with no animation.
- **No height transition on the slot.** Removed from `site.css`; it would re-introduce per-frame layout.
- **`box-shadow` instead of `filter: drop-shadow()` on the tweet container.** A filter over the cross-origin tweet iframe is recomputed whenever the tweet repaints (the same family of cost as the regression in RATIONALE 039). The container has the tweet's 14 px corners, so the shadow is identical.

## Verification

- **Deterministic measure, independent of machine speed:** distinct layout heights each tweet card passes through during its reveal went from **12 (prod) → 2** (before and after; one card shows 3, from Twitter's late second resize).
- After settling, there are no leftover transforms or clip-paths, no running animations, and no tweet cut off (the slot is 8 px taller than the iframe: the stage's padding).
- Long-animation-frame timing could not show the difference on the development desktop: style and layout totalled 8 ms vs 34 ms, and both runs were dominated by unrelated ~1 s frames (Twitter's script, decoding). The laptop is simply too fast. The real check is the Pixel 10.

## Still open

If the press ripple also feels choppy on phones: limit the displacement filter to the wave's bounding box, and add a ring-only (transform/opacity) tier that phones start on, promoted to refraction only when measured smooth.
