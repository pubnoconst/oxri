## Context

The owner asked how PWA-friendly the site is. Audit:

| Already in place | Missing |
|---|---|
| HTTPS (Render) | a web app manifest |
| a viewport meta tag | app icons (only a 96 px favicon) |
| content-hashed, immutable assets, which make offline caching safe | `theme-color` |
| server-rendered pages | an Apple touch icon |
| a CSP whose `default-src 'self'` already admits a same-origin manifest and service worker | a service worker |

Proposed plan:
1. **Installable** (this PR).
2. **A service worker:** cached-first public pages during Render cold starts, offline reading, an offline page. Never cache signed-in pages or POSTs, and no offline writes.

## Decision

- **The icon is the favicon, scaled up.** The owner first chose a dot-matrix icon (a sliding puzzle mid-solve, drawn by a generator), then said "I take it back, let's scale up the favicon instead".
- **`cmd/icongen`** (`mage icons`) makes every icon from `assets/favicon.png`, so the favicon stays the single source. It's pure Go (`CGO_ENABLED=0`) with Catmull-Rom bicubic scaling, and deterministic.
  - **`icon-192` / `icon-512` ("any"):** the whole favicon, frame included.
  - **`icon-maskable-512` and `apple-touch-icon` (180 px):** without the frame, on the favicon's cream. Android and iOS cut these to their own shapes and would clip a frame unevenly.
    - The frame's rounded corners reach about 10 px in. A first crop of 3 px left dark corner remnants, so the crop is 12 px; "RI" spans about x 19–76 and y 20–75 of 96, so there's a wide margin.
    - The cropped favicon fills 70% of the icon, putting the monogram's box (about 0.55, diagonal about 0.78) inside the maskable 80% safe circle.
  - **Trade-off, stated to the owner:** a 96 → 512 px upscale is slightly soft at full size. A crisp version would mean redrawing the monogram from the font (listed as optional in `NEXT.md`).
- **`/manifest.webmanifest`** is built in Go from the asset handles, so icon URLs carry content hashes and are never written by hand. Its contents:
  - **`id: "/"`,** fixed, so a changed manifest never reads to the browser as a different app;
  - **`start_url` / `scope`:** `/`;
  - **`display`:** `standalone`;
  - **`background_color`:** `#fefaee`, the favicon's cream, so the splash blends with the icon;
  - **`theme_color`:** `#f5f4f0`, the light `--page`;
  - served with `Cache-Control: no-cache`, because browsers re-read it.
- **`<head>`** on every page:
  - `rel="manifest"`;
  - `apple-touch-icon`;
  - `apple-mobile-web-app-title`;
  - `theme-color` with `media` for light (`#f5f4f0`) and dark (`#0f1113`).
- **`theme-toggle.js`:** a manual light/dark switch also sets both `theme-color` tags to the chosen page colour, so the status bar or installed-app title bar follows it.

## Verification

- **Route test:**
  - the manifest parses, with `id` "/", `start_url` "/" and standalone display;
  - every icon URL returns a PNG whose real dimensions match its `sizes`, and a maskable icon exists;
  - the landing page links the manifest, the Apple icon and both `theme-color` tags.
- **Chrome (local server):**
  - the manifest is `application/manifest+json`, and all three icon URLs return 200;
  - `theme-color` was `#f5f4f0` / `#0f1113` by system media, and after a toggle to light both were `#f5f4f0`.
  - Chrome's `beforeinstallprompt` was not observed: it can fire during load, before an injected listener exists, and Chrome also applies engagement heuristics. Installing from prod on a phone is the real check.
- **Icons inspected:** the maskable and Apple icons are clean cream, with no frame remnants and the monogram centred. The 512 px "any" icon is the framed favicon.
