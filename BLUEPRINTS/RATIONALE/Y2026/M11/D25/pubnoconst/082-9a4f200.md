## Context

Asked how the performance looked, Claude listed what `font-display: optional` (079) traded away: a first-time visitor sees the matched fallback fonts, not the site's own typefaces. The owner: "in retrospect, the first-visit look being different does not bode well with me." Claude laid out `swap`, `fallback` and `block` and recommended `swap`; the owner chose it.

## What changed

- **Every face is `font-display: swap` again.** Text is drawn at once in the size-matched fallback, and the site's own typeface replaces it in the same space as soon as it arrives. Every reader, first visit included, sees EB Garamond, Figtree, Playpen Sans and Short Stack.
- **`TestFontFacesAreRegularAndHashed`** requires `swap` on every face.
- **HIG §2** says so, and records the reversal.

## What it costs

- **One relayout frame at load** (65–148 ms of style and layout on the throttled phone profile) as the fonts arrive.
- **Layout shift stays 0.000 on real data.** That was measured with `swap` after the tweet-signature fix (078). On the seeded site a quote's body can re-wrap one line (CLS about 0.007, inside the 0.1 gate).

## Why not the others

- **`fallback`** keeps the fallbacks for a page whose fonts take more than about 3 s, so on a very slow connection the look would still differ.
- **`block`** hides text until the fonts arrive, up to 3 s: slower and less stable.

## Lesson

A choice made for stability changed the site's identity on first visits. It was measured correctly, but it was the wrong trade for this owner, and the trade-off should have been weighed against the design before shipping, not after.
