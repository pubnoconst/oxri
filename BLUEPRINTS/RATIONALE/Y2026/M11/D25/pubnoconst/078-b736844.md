## Context

The owner saw a search bring up the bottom-centre dots, followed by results "after a UI flicker", and asked for results that appear stably, since stability beats peak. Claude traced the flicker to three effects:
1. the page dimming to 60 %;
2. a full navigation;
3. the browser's cross-fade overlapping the new page's own fade-in.

The owner chose in-place results. Then, recalling that the dimming had existed to acknowledge a tap, they proposed removing it, loading pages near-instantly with htmx fetching the data afterwards, and an "expand" animation.

Claude pushed back on two parts, with evidence:
- **A frame first, then the data, measured slower:** that was `/journals` before 076, 0.9 s later.
- **Animating height** relays the page out on every frame, which is the stutter §1.6 rules out.

The owner agreed, asked whether content could "ease in" instead, and asked for the recommended set, measured before and after.

## What changed

- **HIG §1.7 "Pages arrive whole and still":**
  - no sudden displacement;
  - new pages ease in at once, as one response;
  - late content eases in, by opacity, within reserved space, never by animating height;
  - every tap is acknowledged at once by the control itself.

  §6 and §7 were rewritten to match: no dimming; arrivals never displace; X's own embed, whose height is unknown, is the one exception.
- **In-place results.**
  - The filter bar and the pager carry `hx-boost` with `hx-select="#journals-catalog-stage"`, so only the results are swapped, in a single frame, and the URL is pushed.
  - The header, the search box (focus and caret, by id) and the scroll position stay.
  - The results stay until replaced, and dim only after 200 ms.
  - A failed request loads the page instead, so a search ends in results or the offline page.
  - The cards' own actions are not boosted.
- **Tap acknowledgement.** The tapped link or button turns `.is-pending` at once, and the dots follow after 180 ms. Page dimming is removed, and with it the dead `.page-dot-loading` and the old catalog-offline message.
- **Reserved media space.**
  - `views/journals/mediakind.go` mirrors `journal-media.js`, so the server knows what each link becomes: video 16:9, audio the player's height, an image a fixed frame with the image fitted whole.
  - A plain link's card is drawn by the server.
- **A clipping's signature is one line.**
- **The lab** scrolls each page to the end. It reports the shift and long frames during the scroll, and `-debug` names each shift's elements and splits each long frame.

## Evidence

`mage benchmark`, 10 cold budget-phone runs, real data, `/journals`:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| CLS | 0.015 on every run | 0.000 on every run |
| Long frames while scrolling | 1 in 5 of 10 runs (51–68 ms) | 0 in 10 |
| LCP median | 1,608 ms | 1,642 ms (within spread) |

The media reservation alone did not move CLS. `-debug` then named the culprit: the signature line `span.tweet-author` was 33 px tall in the fallback and 17 px once Short Stack arrived, moving the card below 10 px.

In Chrome against real data, a search for "god" (10 → 5 entries), the "God" tag (→ 3) and Back all happened without a page load, with no shift, no long frames, and focus and scroll kept.
