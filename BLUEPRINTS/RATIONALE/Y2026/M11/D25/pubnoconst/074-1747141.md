## Context

Asked to judge the UX against the state of the art, Claude listed what is behind:
- the shell-then-fetch catalog;
- the 220 ms page fade-in;
- fonts that swap in;
- no Speculation Rules;
- the double-click theme toggle;
- no `content-visibility`;
- text wrapping.

The owner answered with their rationale: **"stable FPS is better than higher peak FPS."**

## The principle

A steady frame rate is worth more than a higher best case, and a predictable wait is worth more than a sometimes-faster one. A reader on a budget phone notices a stutter or a jump more than they notice a page that could have been 100 ms quicker. Performance work is therefore judged first by the variance it adds or removes, on the A02s, and only then by its best-case speed.

## What it decided

| Proposal | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Speculation Rules prerendering | ruled out | Instant only when it guesses right; otherwise background CPU and memory that can stutter the page being read on an A02s. Peak, not stable. |
| `content-visibility: auto` | ruled out | Moves layout into scrolling, where stutter shows; wrong size estimates make the scrollbar jump. |
| 220 ms page fade-in | kept | A fixed, predictable, opacity-only cost: every page arrives the same way. |
| Cold starts (Render free plan) | first priority | The largest variance on the site: sometimes instant, sometimes a long wake. |
| Self-hosted fonts, size-matched fallbacks, key face preloaded | second | Removes text reflowing at network-dependent moments. It also routes all Greek to one face. |
| Catalog's first page in the first response | third | One paint instead of a later pop-in and layout shift; no blank catalog without JavaScript. htmx stays for search, tags and paging. |
