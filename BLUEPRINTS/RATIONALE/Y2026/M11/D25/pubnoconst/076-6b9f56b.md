## Context

After 075 the owner asked: "are we really failing performance standards in a simple blog like this? Do we need APM? … Is it over?" Claude's answer was no. Only `/journals` was near Google's LCP line, and only on the harsh budget-phone profile. APM, tracing and synthetics don't fit a single-process site.

The owner asked for:
1. a `mage benchmark` against real data and Google's standard;
2. finding `/journals`' bottleneck;
3. a brainstorm of the way out.

They also asked whether fonts or htmx were to blame, comparing a catalog elsewhere that "loads instantly". Then: "go and make page 1 the journal".

## What the benchmark found

`/journals`' LCP element is the first entry's excerpt (text), so all of its wait was render delay, 2,122 ms of 2,274.

1. The page sent an empty frame, and htmx requested `/journals/content` only once the frame and its scripts had run, at about 1.38 s.
2. The catalog arrived at about 1.77 s.
3. A deliberate 120 ms swap pause and the layout of 12 cards followed.

The font was not the cause: the excerpt was drawn in the size-matched fallback, and Playpen arrived later without a shift.

## What changed

- **`handleJournalsCatalog`** renders `journals.CatalogPage` with the filter bar and this page's cards in the first response. That covers search, tags and every page, from the in-memory snapshot.
- **Removed:** `/journals/content`, `CatalogShellPage` and the now-unused `loading.Dots`. Tests read `/journals`.
- **`mage benchmark`** builds the server, runs it signed out against the real database (read-only; local views are never field data) and runs 10 cold budget-phone loads per page with `-breakdown`. The breakdown splits LCP into TTFB, resource wait, resource load and render delay, and adds the LCP element, the catalog request, the last font, and kB / requests. The report goes to `bin/benchmark.md`.

## Result

| `/journals`, 10 runs, real data | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| LCP median ± spread (worst) | 2274 ± 800 ms (2972) | 1498 ± 872 ms (2320) |
| Catalog request | 1382 → 1765 ms | none |
| Last font | 2961 ms | 2335 ms |

The remaining spread is one run with a 993 ms TTFB. The catalog now also works without JavaScript.

## Found while profiling the next step (for the next PR)

- **Production compresses; the local server does not.** Cloudflare in front of Render serves assets with Brotli, which is about 3× smaller. The local lab therefore overstates CSS and JS transfer time.
- **A cold start took 12.6 s** for the live site to answer `/healthz`. That outweighs every front-end figure.
- **htmx is now only used by admin controls** (delete prompts, batch actions, passkey removal), yet every reader downloads and parses it.
- **Assets are not cached at the edge** (`cf-cache-status: DYNAMIC`), although their URLs are content-hashed and immutable.
